A PRACTICAL GUIDE TO OUR FREE ONLINE 2D CAD

From your first line.
To your next DXF.

A drafting workspace for laser-cut profiles, bend layouts, straight-cut blanks and everyday 2D geometry. This reference describes the tools that are actually implemented in Sachin CAD.

Open the drawing workspace ↗

A first drawing in a minute.

  1. Open Sachin CAD and choose New for a blank drawing. The first visit shows a labelled, editable example.
  2. In the command field, enter PLINE 0,0 120,0 120,80 0,80 C to create a closed rectangular profile.
  3. Enter CIRCLE 25,25 5 to create a circle with a 5-unit radius.
  4. Choose Zoom extents or enter ZE. Select a profile edge to edit its properties or drag a square grip.
  5. Use the Layers panel to separate cut profiles, bend lines, dimensions and construction geometry.
  6. Choose Export DXF, confirm units and export scope, then download the file. Save an editable project separately to preserve the full document.

Precision at the command line.

Model coordinates use X to the right and Y upwards. The internal physical unit is millimetres. Switching display units to inches converts coordinate entry and readouts while preserving physical size. DXF export has its own explicit unit choice.

  • 100,50 is an absolute coordinate in the displayed units.
  • @100,50 is a relative displacement from the previous construction point.
  • @100<45 is a relative polar point, 100 units away at 45° counterclockwise from positive X.
  • A plain positive number enters the circle/polygon radius or the ellipse minor-axis distance when requested. For line chains it uses the current cursor direction.
  • Enter finishes a line, polyline or spline chain. C closes it; U removes its last uncommitted point. Escape cancels the active construction.

Selection, grips and navigation.

Click an edge to select it; Shift-click adds or removes objects. Drag selected geometry to move it. Drag square grips to edit endpoints, polyline vertices, circle radius, ellipse axes or Bezier anchors and handles. Grouped objects select together; locked layers cannot be edited.

A left-to-right window selects fully contained objects. A right-to-left window also selects geometry crossing its border. Middle-mouse drag or holding Space pans. The mouse wheel zooms around the pointer. On touch screens, use the Pan tool or two fingers to pan and pinch to zoom. Expand the workspace for more drawing space.

Snaps that support exact work.

Object snaps include endpoints, midpoints, centres, quadrants and nearby intersections. Grid snapping uses the displayed step in current units. Object snaps take precedence over grid and direction constraints. Right-click SNAP for individual snap-mode settings. The visible grid adapts to zoom; the actual snap step remains the value in the status bar.

  • F3: toggle snapping. F7: toggle the visible grid.
  • F8: horizontal/vertical constraint. F10: 45° polar tracking. These direction modes are mutually exclusive.
  • Ctrl/Cmd+Z: undo. Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y: redo.
  • Ctrl/Cmd+S: download a project. Ctrl/Cmd+O: import. Ctrl/Cmd+A: select all unlocked visible objects when outside a text field.
  • Ctrl/Cmd+C/V: copy and paste within the workspace, using an offset copy. This does not read the system clipboard.
  • Delete: erase the selection. Ctrl/Cmd+P: open the print drawing view.

Layers with a manufacturing purpose.

CUT contains profiles. BEND contains fold locations and uses dashed lines by default. ANNOTATION holds text, dimensions, leaders and hatch lines. CONSTRUCTION holds reference points or helper geometry. Add custom layers, change their purpose and colour, and control visibility, line style and locking.

A full DXF export includes all visible layers. The optional cut and bend geometry only scope excludes annotation, construction, text and dimensions. A selected-only scope is also available. Hatching creates actual editable lines on ANNOTATION. Grouping controls editing and selection; exported geometry is expanded rather than written as reusable block definitions.

File formats and exact scope.

  • ASCII DXF import: LINE, CIRCLE, ARC, LWPOLYLINE/POLYLINE including bulges, full ELLIPSE, POINT, basic TEXT/MTEXT, and supported SPLINE geometry. INSERT blocks are expanded, including nested transforms and simple row/column arrays. DIMENSION graphics can be read when their referenced block exists.
  • DXF export: native LINE, CIRCLE, ARC, LWPOLYLINE with bulges, ELLIPSE, POINT, TEXT and cubic SPLINE segments, using a DXF 2007-compatible header. Displayed dimensions export as editable line/text graphics, not associative DIMENSION entities.
  • Curve handling: unmodified circles, arcs and rounded polyline bulges retain their native definitions. Boolean operations, general closed-profile offsets and smoothing use Bezier geometry. General non-rational imported splines are sampled to approximately 0.01 mm before unit/block scaling; four-control-point cubic Beziers remain curves. Partial ellipses are sampled to polylines. Non-uniform transforms can convert curves into Bezier paths.
  • Import reports: unsupported or invalid entity types are listed where detected. Rational splines are skipped because the parser does not preserve their weights. Review imported geometry; this is not a lossless converter for arbitrary CAD files.
  • Other output: SVG vectors and an editable .sachincad.json project. Printing fits the drawing to a page; it is not a verified 1:1 plot.
  • Working limits: 12,000 entities, 100 layers, 250,000 control points and 20 MB source files. Performance depends on your device. Undo history is bounded to 80 states and approximately 32 MB. Local recovery is limited by browser storage; a downloaded project is the durable copy.
  • Outside this editor's scope: native DWG, binary DXF, 3D solids, parametric/geometric constraint solving, xrefs, AutoLISP, complex text formatting, paper-space layouts, associative dimensions, professional true-shape nesting, CAM toolpaths and machine control.

The single-bend flat-layout estimate.

Enter outside leg lengths A and B measured to the theoretical sharp outside intersection, the inside radius R, thickness t, K-factor K and bend angle θ. In this tool, θ = 0° means flat and θ = 90° is a right-angle bend. Values must remain between 0° and 180°.

Bend allowance = θ radians × (R + K × t)
Outside setback = (R + t) × tan(θ / 2)
Bend deduction = 2 × setback − allowance
Flat length = A + B − deduction

The generated rectangle is the estimated blank. Its dashed BEND line is the centre of the developed bend zone. Actual production depends on material, tooling, springback, grain direction and shop-specific bend data. Confirm the flat pattern with the fabricator before cutting.

Review before manufacturing.

The Review panel checks cut-geometry extents, unmatched endpoints within 0.01 mm, whole duplicate objects and zero-length geometry. It compares overall bounds with a selected sheet and the stated 8,000 × 2,500 mm laser bed. It does not prove contour validity, subtract holes from a material-area calculation, detect every overlap/self-intersection, nest parts, compensate kerf or produce a machine program.

At Sachin Industries, our 12 kW fiber laser has headline capacity up to 40 mm. Bending and shearing reach stated capacities up to 25 mm and 8 metres. Maximum thickness and length depend on the material and job and must be confirmed together. See the capability sheet ↗

Your drawing stays with you.

Drafting, import, export and recovery run on your device. The editor does not upload drawings to Sachin Industries or another CAD service. Optional consent-controlled analytics measures tool visits and file-format choices without drawing contents, file names or coordinates. The homepage factory map contacts Google only after you choose to load it. Download a project for a lasting copy; clearing browser storage removes local recovery.

For a manufacturing enquiry, export the file and attach it to an email you send to sachinindustries1660@gmail.com. The website does not submit drawings automatically.
